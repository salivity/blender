How to Twist a Curve Point in Blender

Twisting curve points in Blender—technically known as adjusting control point tilt—allows you to rotate the orientation of a curve's profile along its length. This article explains how to quickly twist individual or multiple curve points using keyboard shortcuts, precise numerical inputs in the N-panel, and how to adjust global curve settings to fix unwanted distortion.

Method 1: Use the Tilt Shortcut (Ctrl + T)

  1. Select your curve object and press Tab to enter Edit Mode.
  2. Left-click to select the control point you want to twist.
  3. Press Ctrl + T (or Cmd + T on macOS) to activate the Tilt tool.
  4. Move your mouse to interactively adjust the twist angle, or type a precise degree value (e.g., 45 or 90) directly on your keyboard.
  5. Press Left-Click or Enter to confirm the transformation.

Tip: Press Alt + T to reset the tilt of selected control points back to zero.

Method 2: Adjust Tilt via the Sidebar (N-Panel)

  1. In Edit Mode, select the target curve control point.
  2. Press N on your keyboard to open the Sidebar menu if it is not visible.
  3. Click on the Item tab at the top of the Sidebar.
  4. Locate the Tilt field under the Transform section.
  5. Click and drag the value to adjust the angle, or click the field to type an exact degree.

Fixing Twist Artifacts (Twist Method)

If twisting a point creates sharp kinks or unexpected deformation along your mesh, change how Blender calculates the curve's rotation profile:

  1. Select the curve object and open the Object Data Properties tab (green curve icon) in the Properties panel.
  2. Expand the Shape section.
  3. Find the Twist Method dropdown menu and switch between the available options:
    • Z-Up: Ideal for horizontal curves; keeps the twist relative to the global Z-axis.
    • Minimum: Smooths out sharp twists along complex 3D paths to prevent flipping.
    • Tangent: Calculates twist based on the vector directional flow of the curve.
