Interpolated Children in Blender Hair Explained

Interpolated children in Blender’s hair particle system are dynamically generated child strands that calculate their position, shape, and rotation based on multiple neighboring parent guide hairs. This feature allows 3D artists to create dense, realistic fur, hair, and grass while styling only a minimal number of parent strands, significantly improving viewport performance and workflow efficiency.

What Are Child Particles?

In Blender, styling individual hairs for an entire scalp or animal coat is computationally expensive and tedious. To solve this, particle systems use parent hairs (guide strands you can style and comb) and child hairs (additional strands generated automatically around the parents).

Blender offers two main types of child particles: Simple and Interpolated.

How Interpolated Children Work

Unlike Simple children, which duplicate and cluster around a single parent strand, Interpolated children calculate their properties across the surface between several adjacent parent guides.

  • Triangulation: Blender takes three or four nearby parent hairs and interpolates (blends) their properties—such as length, curvature, and direction—to position the child hair seamlessly between them.
  • Surface Distribution: Interpolated children distribute evenly across the entire surface of the emitting mesh rather than forming isolated clusters around parent roots.
  • Smooth Transitions: If one parent strand points left and an adjacent one points right, the interpolated child hairs between them will gradually shift direction from left to right, creating natural flow and volume.

Key Advantages of Interpolated Children

  • Optimized Performance: You only need to guide a few dozen or hundred parent hairs in Particle Edit mode, while rendering tens or hundreds of thousands of child hairs at render time.
  • Natural Hair and Fur Coverage: Because the children span the gaps between parent guides, they provide uniform coverage ideal for human hair, animal coats, rugs, and grass.
  • Hairstyle Parting: Interpolated mode includes specific settings to define hair parts, ensuring child strands split correctly along a scalp line rather than crossing over each other unnaturally.

Key Settings to Control Interpolated Children

  • Display and Render Amount: Controls how many child strands are visible in the viewport versus the final render, saving computing power during editing.
  • Clumping: Forces child strands to taper together toward their tips or roots, simulating wet hair, locks, or fur tufts.
  • Roughness and Kink: Adds noise, curl, wave, or frizz specifically to the child strands without altering the underlying parent guides.
  • Parting: Determines how child strands behave near areas where parent strands point in drastically different directions, such as a hair part.
