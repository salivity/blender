What Is the Mesh Filter Tool in Blender?

The Mesh Filter tool in Blender is a specialized Sculpt Mode feature that applies global sculpt deformations to an entire 3D mesh or a masked region all at once. Unlike standard sculpting brushes that only affect the surface directly beneath the cursor, the Mesh Filter affects the whole model uniformly based on the selected filter type, such as smoothing, inflating, or randomizing vertices. This article explains how the Mesh Filter tool works, its primary filter modes, and how to integrate it into your sculpting workflow.

How the Mesh Filter Tool Works

The Mesh Filter operates on the geometry of your active object while in Sculpt Mode. Instead of clicking and brushing over specific areas, you activate the tool and click-and-drag anywhere in the 3D Viewport. Dragging to the right increases the effect’s intensity, while dragging to the left reverses or decreases it.

If a mask is applied to the object, the Mesh Filter will only affect the unmasked regions, allowing for precise controlled deformations across complex shapes.

Key Filter Types

Blender offers several filter types within the Mesh Filter tool, accessible from the top Tool Settings bar:

  • Smooth: Blends vertex positions together to remove harsh details, noise, or rough brushwork across the entire surface.
  • Surface Smooth: Reduces surface noise while preserving larger shape contours and sharp feature edges better than standard smoothing.
  • Inflate: Pushes vertices outward along their normals, adding volume to the mesh globally.
  • Deflate: Pulls vertices inward, shrinking the overall volume of the mesh.
  • Scale: Resizes the mesh vertices relative to the object center or active pivot point.
  • Sphere: Morphs the overall shape of the mesh toward a perfect sphere.
  • Randomize: Applies random displacement noise to the vertices, useful for adding organic textures, rough stone surfaces, or stylized detail.
  • Relax: Adjusts vertex distribution across the mesh surface to make topology more uniform without significantly altering the overall volume.
  • Erase Deformation: Restores vertices back to their state prior to the current sculpt session or shape key base.

How to Use the Mesh Filter Tool

  1. Select your object and switch to Sculpt Mode using the mode selector in the top-left corner or pressing Tab.
  2. Locate and click the Mesh Filter icon in the left-hand toolbar (or press Shift + W).
  3. In the top Tool Settings bar, choose your desired Filter Type from the dropdown menu.
  4. Click anywhere in the 3D Viewport and drag horizontally to apply the effect strength interactively.
  5. Release the mouse button to confirm the deformation.

Common Use Cases

  • Cleaning Up Sculpted Models: Apply a low-strength Smooth or Surface Smooth filter to quickly eliminate unwanted brush noise across an entire sculpt.
  • Creating Base Shapes: Use the Inflate or Sphere filters on primitive shapes to quickly block out stylized character bases or organic forms.
  • Targeted Adjustments with Masks: Draw a mask over specific areas (like a character’s arm or armor piece) and use the Inflate filter to adjust thickness evenly without manual brushing.
