Surface Tension in Blender Fluid Simulations

Surface tension in Blender's Mantaflow fluid simulation system is a physical property that governs the cohesive forces pulling liquid molecules together at the surface. This article explains how the surface tension setting functions within a fluid domain, its direct impact on the behavior, shape, and stability of small droplets, and how to configure it effectively to achieve realistic liquid effects.

Understanding Surface Tension in Mantaflow

In real-world physics, surface tension is the tendency of liquid surfaces to shrink into the minimum possible surface area due to molecular cohesion. Within Blender, this property is simulated inside the Liquid Domain settings under the Liquid > Viscosity / Surface Tension panel.

When enabled, Blender calculates an inward pulling force along the fluid mesh boundaries. Without surface tension, the simulator treats the fluid primarily based on momentum, gravity, and external obstacles, often causing thin sheets of fluid to break apart into chaotic, unnatural particle sprays.

How Surface Tension Affects Small Droplets

The surface tension property fundamentally changes how small droplets generate, behave, and interact during a simulation:

  • Beading and Spherical Shapes: Higher surface tension forces detached fluid particles to round out into defined, spherical droplets (beads) rather than flat or irregular mesh clusters.
  • Suppression of Excessive Spray: When fluid splashes or collides with an obstacle, high surface tension resists breakup. Instead of scattering into disconnected droplets or mist, the fluid forms cohesive tendrils, sheets, or larger consolidated drops.
  • Droplet Coalescence: When two small droplets come into close proximity, strong surface tension pulls them together to merge into a single larger drop, accurately mimicking the behavior of water or mercury.
  • Drip and Pinch-Off Dynamics: As liquid drips from a source, surface tension governs the "necking" phase—the stretching and eventual snapping of the fluid stream into individual falling drops followed by small satellite droplets.

Key Considerations for Setup

To achieve accurate droplet behavior using surface tension in Blender, consider the following parameters:

  • Domain Scale: Surface tension is a scale-dependent force that dominantly affects small-scale phenomena. Ensure the domain's physical dimensions (Simulation Scale in meters) reflect a small volume; large-scale ocean or river simulations do not visually benefit from surface tension.
  • Simulation Resolution: Surface tension requires sufficient grid resolution to resolve thin fluid bridges and tiny spheres. Low domain resolutions may result in jittering, loss of volume, or blocky droplet shapes.
  • Value Calibration: Setting the value too low results in a watery mist with no droplet cohesion, while setting it too high can create an unnatural, rubbery elasticity where the fluid refuses to detach or splash properly.
