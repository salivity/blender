What Are Bezier Curve Control Points in Blender?

This article provides a quick overview of control points on Bezier curves in Blender, explaining what they are, how their handles function, and how they are used to shape curves. You will learn about the different handle types available in Blender and how adjusting these points gives you precise control over 3D paths, modeling, and animation.

Understanding Control Points

In Blender, a Bezier curve is a parametric curve defined by a series of control points. A single control point consists of three distinct parts:

  1. The Central Vertex: The main point located directly on the curve path. Moving this vertex relocates that specific segment of the curve in 3D space.
  2. Two Handles (Control Handles): Extending outward from both sides of the central vertex, these handles dictate the direction, steepness, and curvature of the line as it enters and leaves the vertex.

By manipulating the angle and length of these handles, you alter the shape of the curve without adding extra geometry. Longer handles pull the curve farther, creating a wider arc, while shorter handles create tighter bends.

Handle Types in Blender

Blender offers four primary handle types for Bezier curve control points, each serving a specific modeling or animation purpose. You can change handle types in Edit Mode by selecting a control point and pressing V.

  • Automatic (Yellow): Blender automatically calculates smooth, continuous curvature based on neighboring control points.
  • Vector (Green): Handles point directly at the adjacent control points, creating straight lines and sharp corners.
  • Aligned (Pink/Purple): The two handles remain locked in a straight line relative to each other. Rotating one side rotates the other, ensuring a smooth transition, but you can scale their lengths independently.
  • Free (Red): The two handles act completely independently. This allows you to create sharp angles and asymmetric curves at a single vertex.

Working with Control Points in Edit Mode

To edit control points, select your Bezier curve and press Tab to enter Edit Mode. Standard transform shortcuts apply:

  • Move (G): Translates the central vertex or an individual handle.
  • Rotate (R): Rotates the handles around the central vertex to change the direction of the curve.
  • Scale (S): Extends or shortens the handles to adjust the influence of the curve's bend.
  • Extrude (E): Extrudes a selected control point to add a new segment to the curve.

Control points are essential for defining camera paths, generating sweep geometry with bevel objects, creating motion paths in animations, and designing smooth 2D and 3D shapes.
