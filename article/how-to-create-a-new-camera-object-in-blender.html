How to Create a New Camera Object in Blender

Adding a new camera object in Blender is a fundamental step for framing scenes, setting up rendering angles, and creating animations. This guide covers the quick steps to insert a camera into your 3D Viewport, align it to your perspective, and set it as the active scene camera.

Step 1: Add a New Camera

  1. Open your Blender project and ensure you are in the 3D Viewport.
  2. Place the 3D Cursor where you want the camera to be spawned (by default, it will spawn at the origin or wherever your cursor is located).
  3. Press Shift + A to open the Add menu.
  4. Hover over Camera and left-click.

A new camera object will instantly appear in your scene.

Step 2: Position and Align the Camera

Once the camera is created, you can move and rotate it like any other object, or automatically align it to your current viewing angle:

  • Align Camera to View: Press Ctrl + Alt + Numpad 0 to instantly snap the selected camera to your current viewport perspective.
  • Manual Navigation (Fly/Walk Mode): Select the camera, press Numpad 0 to look through it, then press **Shift + ** (backtick) to navigate the camera using standard WASD` controls and your mouse.
  • Lock Camera to View: Open the sidebar menu by pressing N, go to the View tab, and check the box for Camera to View. Now, rotating and panning the viewport will move the camera directly.

Step 3: Set the Active Camera

Blender scenes can contain multiple cameras, but only one can be active for rendering at a time.

  1. Select the new camera in the 3D Viewport or Outliner.
  2. Press Ctrl + Numpad 0 to set the selected camera as the active camera.
  3. Alternatively, go to the Properties panel on the right, click the Scene tab (funnel/cone icon), and choose your camera from the Camera dropdown menu.

Step 4: Adjust Camera Settings

To tweak lens parameters, select your camera and click the green Object Data Properties (camera icon) tab in the Properties editor. From here, you can change:

  • Lens Type: Switch between Perspective, Orthographic, or Panoramic.
  • Focal Length: Adjust the millimeter value to change the field of view (e.g., 35mm for wide shots, 85mm for portraits).
  • Depth of Field: Enable depth of field and select a focus target to achieve background blur (bokeh).
