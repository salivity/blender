What Does the Multi-Plane Scrape Brush Do in Blender?

The Multi-Plane Scrape brush in Blender is a powerful sculpting tool designed primarily for hard-surface modeling and stylized character creation. Unlike standard scraping tools that flatten geometry along a single flat surface, the Multi-Plane Scrape brush flattens mesh geometry across two intersecting planes at a predefined angle. This allows artists to quickly form clean bevels, sharp edges, and defined angled corners directly onto organic or rough 3D sculpts.

How the Multi-Plane Scrape Brush Works

When you draw a stroke across a mesh, the Multi-Plane Scrape brush evaluates the surface normals and applies two flat scraping planes that join at a specific angle along the stroke's path.

As you drag the brush along an existing ridge or corner, it simultaneously flattens both sides of that ridge. The intersection of these two planes creates a sharp, well-defined edge. If applied to a flat or organic surface, it carves out a crevice or V-shaped notch matching the set angle.

Key Settings and Controls

To get the most out of the Multi-Plane Scrape brush, you need to understand its core settings located in the Brush Tool Properties panel:

  • Plane Angle: This is the most critical setting. It determines the angle between the two scraping planes (measured in degrees). A 90-degree setting creates right-angle corners, while wider angles produce subtle bevels, and narrower angles carve steep channels.
  • Dynamic Mode: When enabled, the brush continuously updates its plane orientation relative to the underlying geometry as you draw the stroke, making it follow curved edges smoothly.
  • Hard Edge: Toggles whether the peak of the intersection remains crisp or gets slightly smoothed out during the stroke.
  • Area Radius: Controls how far out from the stroke path the scraping effect extends, allowing for wide chamfers or tight detail work.

Primary Use Cases in Blender Sculpting

Hard-Surface Sculpting

Sculpting mechanical objects, armor, vehicles, or architectural elements often requires sharp corners and flat panels. The Multi-Plane Scrape brush eliminates the need to manually flatten adjacent faces, allowing you to quickly block out crisp metallic or stone shapes.

Stylized Art and Anime Characters

Stylized 3D models—such as characters seen in modern animated films or video games—rely heavily on clean planes and defined edge flow on hair, jawlines, and clothing folds. This brush makes it easy to carve angular, planar shapes into stylized sculpts.

Reinforcing Soft Edges

During the digital sculpting process, resolution changes (like using Dynamic Topology or Multiresolution) can cause sharp edges to become soft or rounded. Dragging the Multi-Plane Scrape brush along these edges instantly restores their crispness.
