Blender Floor Constraint Offset in Footwear Rigging

This article provides an overview of how the Floor constraint's Offset property functions during the character footwear rigging process in Blender. It explains how this parameter establishes ground clearance, compensates for varying shoe sole thicknesses, and enables seamless footwear swapping on a single character rig without requiring destructive modifications to the underlying armature.

Understanding the Floor Constraint and Offset

The Floor constraint in Blender restricts the movement of a bone or object along a designated axis, preventing it from passing through a target surface, such as the ground plane.

By default, the constraint uses the bone's origin point as the contact limit. The Offset property introduces a numerical distance buffer along the constraint axis, effectively raising or lowering the stopping point relative to the target plane.

Compensating for Sole Thickness

When a character rig is built, foot Inverse Kinematics (IK) controllers and foot bones are typically aligned with the flat base of a bare foot. However, adding footwear introduces extra geometry—such as rubber outsoles, foam midsoles, or elevated heels—below the original foot level.

Without adjusting the constraint, the shoe sole would sink into the floor mesh until the bone origin reaches the ground. Setting a positive Offset value shifts the stop limit downward relative to the bone, forcing the IK controller to rest above the ground plane by the exact thickness of the shoe sole. This ensures the bottom of the shoe geometry rests cleanly on the floor without clipping or floating.

Managing Asymmetrical Footwear and High Heels

Footwear with uneven elevation, such as high-heeled shoes, requires distinct ground limits for the ball of the foot and the heel. Riggers use the Offset property on separate foot-roll or toe and heel bones:

  • Heel Bone Constraints: Assigned a larger offset value corresponding to the heel height.
  • Toe/Ball Constraints: Assigned a smaller offset value matching the front platform thickness.

This separation prevents the heel from clipping into the ground while keeping the toe grounded properly during natural foot-roll animation cycles.

Facilitating Dynamic Footwear Swapping

In production pipelines where characters change outfits, the Offset property eliminates the need to create separate armatures for different shoes.

Riggers often connect the Offset property to custom properties on the rig using Drivers. When an animator switches the character's footwear from flat sneakers to boots or heels, the driver automatically updates the Offset value, instantly calibrating the rig's floor collision limits to the newly selected shoe model.
