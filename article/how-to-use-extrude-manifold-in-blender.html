How to Use Extrude Manifold in Blender

The Extrude Manifold tool in Blender allows 3D artists to extrude faces inward or along existing geometry without creating messy, overlapping faces or non-manifold geometry. This guide explains how to find, activate, and effectively use the Extrude Manifold tool in Edit Mode, detailing its advantages over standard extrusions and key settings to keep your mesh clean.

What is Extrude Manifold?

Unlike the standard Extrude Region tool (E), which simply creates new faces regardless of intersecting geometry, Extrude Manifold automatically cleans up overlapping edges and dissolves adjacent geometry when pushing a face inward or sliding it along neighboring faces. This helps maintain a clean, manifold mesh structure without requiring manual cleanup like deleting interior faces or merging vertices.

How to Access the Tool

You can access the Extrude Manifold tool in Blender using two main methods:

Method 1: The Toolbar

  1. Switch to Edit Mode by pressing Tab.
  2. Ensure you are in Face Select Mode (press 3 on your keyboard).
  3. On the left Toolbar (T to toggle visible), click and hold the Extrude Region icon.
  4. Select Extrude Manifold from the pop-up menu.

Method 2: The Extrude Menu Shortcut

  1. Select the face or faces you want to modify in Edit Mode.
  2. Press Alt + E to open the Extrude Menu.
  3. Click Extrude Manifold.

Step-by-Step Usage Guide

  1. Select the Face: Click on the face you wish to push inward or drag across adjacent faces.
  2. Activate the Tool: Use Alt + E and select Extrude Manifold, or click the gizmo handle if using the Toolbar tool.
  3. Perform the Extrusion: Drag the face along its normal (or along adjacent edges).
    • When pushing inward into a mesh, notice how adjacent side faces dissolve automatically instead of creating internal geometry.
    • When pushing past an edge or corner, Blender recalculates the surrounding topology to maintain a closed, valid mesh.

Tool Settings in the Adjust Last Operation Panel

After completing an extrusion, expand the Extrude Elements and Move panel in the bottom-left corner of the 3D Viewport to fine-tune your result:

  • Offset: Controls the precise distance of the extrusion.
  • Dissolve Orthogonal Edges: Automatically removes edges that are perpendicular to the direction of movement when they become redundant.
  • Orientation: Choose whether the movement aligns with the Normal, Local, or Global transformation orientation.

When to Use Extrude Manifold vs. Standard Extrude

  • Use Extrude Manifold when cutting pockets, windows, steps, or recesses directly into existing objects (hard-surface modeling).
  • Use Standard Extrude (E) when extending a mesh outward into free space where there is no adjacent geometry to overlap or dissolve.
