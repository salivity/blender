How to Enable Statistics in Blender Viewport

Monitoring polygon counts, vertex totals, and scene performance is essential for 3D modeling, game asset optimization, and rendering efficiency. Blender allows you to display live viewport statistics directly inside the 3D Viewport or along the bottom status bar with just a few clicks. This guide provides quick, step-by-step instructions on how to toggle these statistics on in modern versions of Blender.

Step-by-Step: Enable Viewport Statistics

To display the object and polygon counts directly in the top-left corner of your 3D Viewport:

  1. Open your Blender project and locate the 3D Viewport.
  2. Look at the top-right corner of the viewport screen.
  3. Click the Viewport Overlays drop-down menu (represented by an icon showing a solid circle overlapping a wireframe circle).
  4. Scroll down to the General section of the pop-up menu.
  5. Check the box labeled Statistics.

Once enabled, an overlay will instantly appear in the upper-left corner of your 3D Viewport showing live data for your scene.

Alternative Method: Enable Statistics in the Status Bar

If you prefer a cleaner 3D Viewport without overlay text, you can display scene statistics along the bottom edge of the Blender window:

  1. Move your cursor to the Status Bar at the very bottom right of the Blender interface.
  2. Right-click anywhere on the blank area of the status bar.
  3. Check the Scene Statistics option from the context menu.

This displays total scene data without taking up visible space inside your 3D workspace.

What Information Is Displayed?

When viewport statistics are active, Blender shows real-time information based on your current selection mode (Object Mode or Edit Mode):

  • Objects: The number of selected objects versus the total object count in the scene.
  • Vertices: The number of active/selected vertices.
  • Edges: The total count of active/selected edges.
  • Faces: The total number of active/selected polygons/faces.
  • Triangles: The total triangle count (critical for real-time game engines).
  • Tracks / Memory: Information on motion tracking points or overall memory usage (depending on context).

If you are in Edit Mode, the display dynamically shifts to show selected components relative to the total components on the active mesh object.
