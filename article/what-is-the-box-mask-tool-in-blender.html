What Is the Box Mask Tool in Blender?

The Box Mask tool in Blender is a sculpting utility that allows 3D artists to isolate specific areas of a mesh by drawing a rectangular boundary around them. This quick guide covers what the Box Mask tool does, how to access and use it in Sculpt Mode, essential keyboard shortcuts, and practical applications to improve your digital sculpting workflow.

Understanding the Box Mask Tool

In Blender’s Sculpt Mode, masking is a technique used to protect specific parts of a 3D model from being affected by sculpting brushes. The Box Mask tool creates a rectangular selection area on your mesh. Any vertices falling inside this box become masked, turning a darker shade of gray, which locks them in place so subsequent brush strokes, transforms, or filters will not alter them.

How to Access and Use the Box Mask Tool

You can activate and apply the Box Mask tool using a few simple steps:

  1. Enter Sculpt Mode: Select your 3D mesh and switch from Object Mode to Sculpt Mode using the mode dropdown menu in the top-left corner or by pressing Ctrl + Tab.
  2. Select the Tool: Click the Box Mask icon in the toolbar on the left side of the viewport. Alternatively, press the shortcut key B while in Sculpt Mode.
  3. Apply the Mask: Click and drag your cursor over the area of the mesh you wish to mask. Releasing the mouse button creates the rectangular mask.

Key Controls and Shortcuts for Masking

  • Create Box Mask: Press B, then click and drag over the mesh.
  • Invert Mask: Press Ctrl + I to flip the mask, protecting the unmasked areas and exposing the previously masked region for sculpting.
  • Clear Mask: Press Alt + M to remove all masks from the object.
  • Smooth Mask: Go to the top menu and select Sculpt > Mask > Smooth Mask (or press Ctrl + A depending on keymap settings) to soften the transition edges of the mask.

Common Use Cases in 3D Modeling

  • Protecting Detailed Areas: Lock intricate features, such as facial details or sharp hard-surface edges, while working on adjacent large-scale shapes.
  • Extracting Geometry: Mask a rectangular or shaped area on a character’s body (like a belt or shirt) and use the Mesh Filter or Extract Mask command to instantly generate new clothing objects.
  • Posing and Bending: Combine a box mask with the Pose Brush or Rotate Tool to bend limbs or joints cleanly at specific angles without distorting the rest of the model.
