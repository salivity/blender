Blender Snap Elements Explained

Blender’s snapping functionality allows 3D artists to align objects, vertices, edges, and faces with high precision. This article provides a clear breakdown of all the primary snap elements available in Blender, explaining how each mode functions so you can optimize your modeling workflow and achieve exact placement in your 3D scenes.

What Are Snap Elements?

Snap elements determine the target geometry or grid reference that your active selection (objects, vertices, edges, or faces) will snap to when transforming (moving, rotating, or scaling). You can toggle snapping on or off using the magnet icon at the top of the 3D Viewport or by pressing Shift + Tab.

Below are the key snap elements available in Blender:

1. Increment

  • Function: Snaps your selection to the visible grid increments in the 3D Viewport.
  • Best Used For: Quick, blocky layouts or aligning objects to exact unit distances.
  • Tip: Holding Shift while transforming with Increment active allows for finer, sub-grid precision.

2. Vertex

  • Function: Snaps the selection to the nearest vertex of any object.
  • Best Used For: Precise mesh modeling, combining duplicate vertices, connecting modular assets, or aligning corners exactly.

3. Edge

  • Function: Snaps the selection to any point along an existing edge of a target mesh.
  • Best Used For: Sliding elements along complex structural lines or aligning objects along angled geometry.

4. Face Project

  • Function: Projects the transformed selection directly onto the target face surface.
  • Best Used For: Retopology, placing decals on curved surfaces, or dropping objects directly onto ground terrain.

5. Face Nearest

  • Function: Snaps the selection to the point on a face surface that is closest to the target element.
  • Best Used For: Placing objects seamlessly against walls, floors, or slanted surfaces without sinking through the geometry.

6. Volume

  • Function: Snaps the selection inside the interior volume of a target mesh object.
  • Best Used For: Scattering interior details, filling containers with objects, or positioning lights deep inside transparent or hollow geometry.

7. Edge Center

  • Function: Snaps specifically to the exact midpoint of an edge.
  • Best Used For: Finding perfect centers of structural supports, walls, or mechanical parts without manual measurement.

8. Edge Perpendicular

  • Function: Snaps to the point on an edge that forms a 90-degree (perpendicular) angle relative to the active element.
  • Best Used For: Hard-surface modeling, CAD-style layout design, and creating precise right angles across disconnected geometry.

Snap Target Options

When using snap elements, you can also define which part of your selected object aligns with the target element:

  • Closest: Snaps the part of the selection closest to the target.
  • Center: Snaps the bounding box center of the selection to the target.
  • Median: Snaps the median point (average center) of the selection to the target.
  • Active: Snaps using the active element (the last selected vertex, edge, face, or object) as the reference point.
