Blender New Curve-Based Hair System Explained

This article explores the modern curve-based hair system introduced in Blender 3.3 and expanded in subsequent versions, detailing how it modernizes digital grooming. You will learn about the shift from legacy particle systems to native hair curves, the deep integration with Geometry Nodes for procedural styling, the dedicated interactive grooming brushes, and the performance benefits of this updated workflow.

Overview of the New Hair Architecture

Blender’s new hair system replaces the legacy particle-based hair with a native "Curves" data block. Previously, hair styling was tied to the complex and aging particle system, which suffered from rigid workflows, high memory overhead, and limited editing options. The new system treats hair strands as lightweight curves attached directly to a mesh surface, offering better viewport performance and a vastly improved user experience.

Curves Sculpt Mode and Interactive Grooming

Grooming is handled through the dedicated Curves Sculpt Mode, providing a tactile, brush-based experience similar to digital sculpting. Artists can interactively style, cut, and manipulate hair guides using specialized brushes, including:

  • Comb: Directs and shapes the flow of the hair strands.
  • Density: Adds or removes hair strands procedurally based on brush radius and surface area.
  • Add: Interactively places individual hairs or clusters onto the mesh surface.
  • Snake Hook & Elastic: Pulls, stretches, and creates organic flow in hair guides.
  • Puff & Pinch: Adjusts strand volume and gathers hairs together for clumping effects.
  • Smooth: Softens harsh angles and relaxes hair flow across the scalp.

Procedural Control via Geometry Nodes

The defining feature of the new hair system is its full integration with Geometry Nodes. Instead of relying entirely on manual placement, artists use node-based modifier setups to generate hair density, clumps, frizz, curls, braids, and noise procedurally.

Blender provides built-in Hair Node Assets in the Asset Browser, allowing creators to drag and drop common grooming effects—such as child strand generation and clumping—directly onto the hair object without building complex node trees from scratch. Because this workflow is non-destructive, underlying guide curves can be edited at any time while the procedural nodes automatically update the final look.

Shading, Rendering, and Performance

The curve-based hair system is fully compatible with both the Cycles and EEVEE rendering engines. It works seamlessly with the Principled Hair BSDF shader, allowing accurate simulation of light scattering, melanin absorption, and realistic color distribution. Because the underlying data structure is optimized for modern GPUs and multi-threaded CPUs, scenes containing millions of individual hair strands render faster and consume significantly less memory than the legacy particle system.
