How to Use Anchored Strokes in Blender Sculpting

Anchored strokes in Blender allow you to click, drag, and scale a brush effect or texture directly onto a mesh from a single anchor point. This sculpting technique provides precise control over the placement, size, and rotation of stamps, alphas, and localized mesh deformations. This guide explains how to enable anchored strokes, configure brush settings for optimal results, and apply practical workflows for detailed 3D modeling.

What is an Anchored Stroke?

Unlike standard continuous painting or sculpting strokes, an Anchored stroke method freezes the brush center to the initial point on the mesh where you click. As you drag the mouse or tablet stylus away from that point, the brush size expands, and moving around the anchor point rotates the effect. Releasing the click bakes the deformation or texture into the geometry at that exact spot.

Step-by-Step Guide to Enabling Anchored Strokes

  1. Enter Sculpt Mode: Select your 3D object and switch from Object Mode to Sculpt Mode using the top-left mode dropdown or pressing Tab.
  2. Select a Brush: Choose a sculpting brush suitable for stamping, such as Draw, Clay, Layer, or Inflate.
  3. Open Stroke Settings: Locate the Stroke dropdown panel in the top Tool Settings bar, or open the Sidebar (N key) and navigate to the Tool tab.
  4. Change the Stroke Method: Click the Stroke Method menu (default is usually Space) and select Anchored.

Working with Textures and Alphas

Anchored strokes are most effective when paired with textures or alpha maps to stamp complex details like scales, rivets, skin pores, or bolts.

  1. Go to the Properties panel on the right side of the screen and click the Texture tab at the bottom.
  2. Click New to create a brush texture and load your desired image (e.g., a black-and-white alpha heightmap).
  3. In the Sculpt Tool settings, open the Texture panel and set the Mapping mode to View Plane or Area Plane so the image projects clearly onto the surface.
  4. Left-click on the mesh, drag outward to scale the texture, rotate to align it, and release to apply.

Essential Settings for Anchored Strokes

  • Edge to Edge: Found inside the Stroke panel settings when Anchored is selected. Toggling this option changes the scaling origin from the center of the drag to the outer edge of the brush.
  • Falloff: Controls how the edges of the stroke blend into the surrounding geometry. Use a Smooth falloff for natural organic blending, or a Constant / Square falloff for crisp, hard-surface features.
  • Front Faces Only: Enable this in the Advanced brush settings to prevent the anchored stroke from affecting geometry on the back side of thin objects.
  • Symmetry: Anchored strokes fully support Blender’s X, Y, and Z symmetry settings, allowing you to scale and rotate identical features on both sides of a mesh simultaneously.

Common Use Cases

  • Hard-Surface Details: Placing consistent bolts, panel lines, or screw heads onto mechanical models.
  • Organic Textures: Adding individual wrinkles, pores, scars, or reptile scales precisely where needed.
  • Pattern Placement: Stamping emblems, logos, or localized geometric displacement without smearing the brush path.
