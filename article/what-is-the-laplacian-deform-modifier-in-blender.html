What Is the Laplacian Deform Modifier in Blender?

The Laplacian Deform modifier in Blender is a powerful mesh-modeling tool that allows artists to pose, stretch, and deform 3D geometry while automatically preserving fine surface details. By using differential coordinates—often referred to as Laplacian coordinates—the modifier calculates the local shape of a mesh based on the relationships between adjacent vertices. This article covers how the Laplacian Deform modifier works, its key settings, common use cases, and best practices for applying it to your Blender projects.

How the Laplacian Deform Modifier Works

Unlike standard transformation tools that deform geometry linearly, the Laplacian Deform modifier focuses on preserving local curvature and surface features (such as muscle definition, wrinkles, or panel details). It achieves this by taking a snapshot of the mesh’s original shape during a process called “binding.”

When you move designated control points (anchors), Blender recalculates the surrounding vertex positions to fit the new overall shape while maintaining the relative angles and distances of the original surface details.

Step-by-Step Setup Guide

To use the Laplacian Deform modifier correctly, follow these steps:

  1. Create a Vertex Group: Select the vertices on your mesh that will act as control points (anchors) and assign them to a new Vertex Group.
  2. Add the Modifier: Select your mesh, open the Modifier Properties panel, and add the Laplacian Deform modifier.
  3. Assign the Anchors: Set the Anchors Vertex Group field in the modifier settings to the vertex group you created.
  4. Bind the Mesh: Click the Bind button. This calculates the initial geometric relationships and locks the base shape.
  5. Deform the Geometry: Move, scale, or rotate the anchor vertices (or use a rig linked to those vertices) to deform the mesh smoothy.

Key Modifier Settings

  • Repeat: Controls how many iterations the solver performs. Higher values produce smoother, more natural deformations across the mesh but require more processing power.
  • Anchors Vertex Group: Specifies which vertices serve as the driving handles for the deformation. Unselected vertices in this group remain flexible, while assigned vertices directly drive the deformation.
  • Bind / Unbind: The Bind button captures the original mesh state. If you edit the base geometry or change vertex assignments later, you must click Unbind, adjust your mesh, and click Bind again.

Primary Use Cases

  • Organic Character Posing: Quickly pose body parts, fingers, or facial features without setting up a full armature rig.
  • Detail-Preserving Sculpting Tweaks: Adjust the overall proportions of a sculpted model (like lengthening an arm or twisting a torso) without blurring high-frequency sculpt details.
  • Non-Destructive Mesh Adjustments: Make structural edits to complex models while keeping original surface features intact.

Limitations and Considerations

  • High Computational Cost: The Laplacian solver is computationally expensive. It can cause viewport lag on high-poly models with hundreds of thousands of polygons.
  • Topology Dependencies: You cannot alter the mesh topology (add or remove vertices/faces) while the modifier is bound. Any topological changes require unbinding and rebinding.
  • Extreme Rotations: Pushing anchors through extreme rotations can cause self-intersection or localized volume loss, requiring additional iterations in the Repeat setting to fix.
