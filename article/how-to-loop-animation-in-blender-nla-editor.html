How to Loop Animation in Blender NLA Editor

Looping an animation strip in Blender's Non-Linear Animation (NLA) Editor allows you to repeat walk cycles, background motions, or any keyframed action across a timeline without manually duplicating keyframes. This guide explains how to convert an action into an NLA strip and configure its playback properties to loop seamlessly and continuously.

Step 1: Push Down Your Action to the NLA Editor

  1. Open the Non-Linear Animation editor window.
  2. Ensure your object has an active Action assigned in the Dope Sheet / Action Editor.
  3. In the NLA Editor, click the Push Down button on the active action track, or select the track and press Page Down / click Add > Add Action Strip. This converts the raw keyframes into a tweakable NLA strip.

Step 2: Open the Strip Properties Panel

  1. Click on the NLA strip to select it (it will be highlighted).
  2. Press N on your keyboard to toggle open the right-hand Sidebar/Properties panel.
  3. Select the Strip tab at the top of the sidebar.

Step 3: Increase the Repeat Value

  1. In the sidebar, expand the Action Clip panel.
  2. Locate the Repeat field (found under the Playback Settings section).
  3. Increase the Repeat value:
    • Enter a specific number of cycles (e.g., 5.0 to repeat the animation five times).
    • Set a high number (e.g., 100.0 or more) if you want the strip to loop indefinitely for the duration of a long scene.
  4. The strip on the timeline will visually extend, displaying vertical repeat lines indicating each cycle.

Step 4: Ensure Smooth Transitioning (Optional)

  • Extrapolation: Set Extrapolation to Hold or Hold Forward under the Active Strip panel if you want the object to maintain its state after the loops finish.
  • Blending: Set Blending to Replace or Combine depending on whether this track should override or layer on top of underlying animation tracks.
  • Matching Keyframes: Make sure the first and last keyframe of your original Action match perfectly to avoid a visible jerk or stutter at the loop boundaries.
