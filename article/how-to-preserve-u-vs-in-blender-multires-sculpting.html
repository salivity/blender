How to Preserve UVs in Blender Multires Sculpting

Sculpting with Blender's Multiresolution modifier allows artists to add millions of polygons worth of micro-details while maintaining a lightweight base mesh. However, subdividing and deforming geometry can inadvertently stretch or distort UV coordinates if the proper settings are ignored. This guide provides a straightforward breakdown of how to configure the Multiresolution modifier, utilize UV smoothing options, and maintain clean UV maps throughout your sculpting and baking workflow.

1. Unwrap the Base Mesh First

Always create and finalize your UV layout on the base mesh before adding subdivision levels in the Multiresolution modifier. The modifier relies on the initial 2D coordinates of the original low-poly vertices. Unwrapping the model after sculpting high-resolution details often leads to mapping errors, severe stretching, and difficulty in aligning texture seams.

2. Configure Multiresolution UV Smoothing

When the Multiresolution modifier subdivides geometry, it also subdivides the UV layout. In the Multiresolution modifier panel, adjust the following advanced settings to control how UVs behave:

  • Subdivide UVs: Ensure this option is enabled. It ensures that the UV coordinates are interpolated smoothly to match the curved surface of the subdivided mesh.
  • UV Smooth Mode: Choose the smoothing algorithm that matches your texturing pipeline:
    • Smooth, keep corners: Smooths the UV islands while locking the corner vertices in place. This is the recommended setting for most standard workflows.
    • Smooth, keep boundaries: Smooths interior UV faces while leaving all boundary edges rigid. This prevents UV islands from shrinking away from texture borders.
    • None: Subdivides the mesh without smoothing the UVs at all. Use this if your texturing software uses linear UV interpolation.

3. Never Enable Dynamic Topology (Dyntopo)

Dynamic Topology fundamentally rewrites mesh geometry by dynamically adding and removing vertices on the fly. Enabling Dyntopo automatically discards all existing UV maps on the mesh. To preserve your UV coordinates, strictly use the subdivision levels provided inside the Multiresolution modifier and keep Dyntopo turned off.

4. Avoid Radical Boundary Deformations

While the Multiresolution modifier preserves topology, aggressive sculpting near open borders, seams, or thin geometry can pull the underlying vertices too far from their initial positions. This causes visible texture stretching in the UV editor. Use masks or lower brush strength near UV seams to prevent extreme coordinate distortion.

5. Bake Directly from Multiresolution

When transferring high-poly sculpt details to a texture map, bake directly from the modifier rather than using a separate high-to-low poly ray-casting bake:

  1. Switch your render engine to Cycles.
  2. Set the Multiresolution modifier Preview level to 0 (or your desired low-poly export level) and the Sculpt level to your highest detail level.
  3. Open the Bake panel under Render Properties.
  4. Check Bake from Multires.
  5. Select Normals or Displacement as the bake type, assign a target texture, and click Bake.

This method computes displacement precisely along the evaluated surface without introducing projection artifacts or UV seam misalignments.
