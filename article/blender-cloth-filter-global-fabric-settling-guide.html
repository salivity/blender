Blender Cloth Filter: Global Fabric Settling Guide

The Cloth Filter in Blender's Sculpt Mode enables 3D artists to apply real-time physics-based fabric simulation globally across an entire mesh rather than manipulating vertices with localized brush strokes. By applying operations such as gravity, inflation, and expansion uniformly, this tool instantly creates natural drapery, weight distribution, and realistic folds. This article explains how the Cloth Filter simulates global fabric settling, how it interacts with collision objects, and how to effectively utilize it to achieve realistic cloth behavior on static sculpts.

How the Cloth Filter Works

Unlike standard sculpting brushes that only affect areas under the cursor, the Cloth Filter evaluates the entire topology simultaneously. When activated in Sculpt Mode, clicking and dragging in the viewport dynamically computes cloth solver algorithms across the whole object, adjusting vertex positions based on physical simulation parameters.

Achieving Global Settling with Gravity

To make fabric settle naturally across a sculpt, the Cloth Filter uses the Gravity filter mode. When dragged, the filter applies a directional downward force across all vertices:

  • Mass and Draping: The solver calculates the interaction between vertex mass and structural tension, causing the fabric to hang, stretch, and gather based on its defined stiffness.
  • Natural Tension Points: The mesh develops realistic tension lines radiating from anchor points or high-contact areas, mimicking how real textiles suspend and fall under their own weight.

Collision Detection and Rest Surfaces

Global settling relies heavily on collision detection to prevent the fabric from simply falling infinitely:

  • Collision Geometry: When configured with scene collisions or underlying body meshes, the cloth solver stops vertices upon contact, forcing the excess surface area to bunch up into resting folds, wrinkles, and pleats.
  • Self-Collision: The filter can evaluate self-collision, preventing overlapping cloth surfaces from intersecting as heavy fabric piles on top of itself.

Key Settings for Realistic Settling

To control the behavior of the settling effect, several cloth parameters adjust how the global filter behaves:

  • Cloth Mass: Determines the perceived weight of the material. Higher values produce heavier, larger folds, while lower values yield lighter, finer wrinkles.
  • Cloth Damping: Controls how quickly the energy of the falling fabric dissipates, preventing excessive bouncing or unrealistic stretching during settling.
  • Stiffness (Tension, Compression, Bending): Regulates how resistant the mesh is to stretching and bending, allowing you to emulate stiff leather, heavy denim, or light silk.

Workflow Advantages

Using the Cloth Filter for global settling drastically accelerates character and prop workflows. Instead of setting up a traditional dynamic cloth simulation modifier and baking frames along a timeline, artists can interactively drop, settle, and lock a fabric sculpt into place with a single mouse drag, providing an efficient balance between simulation accuracy and creative control.
