How to Adjust Specular Highlight in Blender

Adjusting the specular highlight in Blender allows you to control how shiny, reflective, and bright a surface appears when light strikes it. Whether you are using the Eevee or Cycles render engine, controlling specular reflections is primarily handled through the Principled BSDF shader using the Specular, Roughness, and Specular Tint parameters. This guide walks you through the step-by-step process of tweaking these settings in the Shader Editor to achieve realistic or stylized material highlights.

Step 1: Open the Shader Editor

  1. Select the 3D object you want to modify in the Viewport.
  2. Switch to the Shading workspace at the top of the Blender window, or open a Shader Editor window.
  3. Ensure your object has a material applied. If it does not, click New at the top of the Shader Editor to create a material with a Principled BSDF node attached to the Material Output.

Step 2: Adjust the Specular Intensity

The Specular value controls the overall brightness and strength of non-metallic reflections.

  • Specular IOR Level (or Specular): Locate the Specular IOR Level slider (labeled as Specular in older Blender versions) on the Principled BSDF node.
  • Decrease the value (towards 0.0): Reduces or removes the light highlight completely, making the material look matte or non-reflective (like cloth or chalk).
  • Increase the value (towards 1.0): Intensifies the reflection of light sources, making the surface look coated or shiny (like plastic or polished wood). The default value of 0.5 represents a standard dielectric specular reflection rate of roughly 4%.

Step 3: Control Highlight Sharpness with Roughness

Specular highlights do not work in isolation; their spread and clarity are directly governed by the Roughness slider.

  • Low Roughness (near 0.0): Focuses the specular highlight into a tight, sharp point, creating a smooth, mirror-like or glossy surface.
  • High Roughness (near 1.0): Spreads the specular highlight over a wider area, creating a soft, diffuse glare typical of rough or textured surfaces.

Step 4: Adjust Specular Tint (Optional)

By default, specular highlights on non-metallic surfaces reflect the color of the light source (usually white).

  • To tint the highlight with the object's base color, increase the Specular Tint slider on the Principled BSDF node. This is useful for stylized art or specific complex materials like subtle iridescent coatings.

Step 5: Use Textures for Variable Highlights

To create realistic surfaces with scratches, fingerprints, or uneven wear, drive the specular highlight using texture maps instead of a single uniform slider value:

  1. Press Shift + A in the Shader Editor and select Texture > Image Texture.
  2. Open a specular or roughness map (a black-and-white image).
  3. Set the image node's Color Space to Non-Color.
  4. Connect the Color output of the Image Texture to the Specular IOR Level or Roughness input of the Principled BSDF node.
  5. (Optional) Insert a Converter > ColorRamp node between the texture and the shader to fine-tune the contrast and intensity of the highlight map.

Understanding Metallic vs. Specular Highlights

If your material's Metallic slider is set to 1.0, the standard Specular controls are bypassed. Metallic surfaces reflect light differently by tinting the entire specular reflection with the material's Base Color. To adjust highlights on metals, change the Base Color, Roughness, or Anisotropic settings rather than the Specular slider.
