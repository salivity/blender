Blender Color Management: AgX vs Filmic Explained

This article provides an overview of Blender's Color Management settings, focusing on how scene-referred linear light is converted into display-ready imagery. It breaks down the core functions of the Color Management tab, explains the mechanics of tone mapping, and compares the Filmic and AgX View Transforms to help you achieve accurate, photorealistic color and dynamic range in your renders.

What Is the Color Management Tab?

The Color Management tab, located under the Render Properties panel in Blender, controls how raw light data calculated by the render engine is translated into colors visible on your monitor.

Blender’s internal render engines (Cycles and EEVEE) calculate light physically using a scene-referred linear workflow. In this space, light values have no upper limit (0.0 to infinity) and light scales linearly. However, standard computer monitors operate in a display-referred space (such as sRGB or Display P3), which can only display values between 0.0 (pure black) and 1.0 (pure white).

The Color Management tab acts as a bridge between these two spaces, providing tone mapping, color space conversion, and artistic grading before the final image is output.

Key Settings in the Panel:

  • Display Device: The color profile of your physical screen (typically set to sRGB).
  • View Transform: The tone-mapping curve used to compress high-dynamic-range data into displayable values (e.g., Standard, Filmic, AgX, False Color).
  • Look: Preset contrast adjustments applied on top of the View Transform (e.g., Medium High Contrast).
  • Exposure and Gamma: Global adjustments for brightness and luminance curves before the final output.

The Role of View Transforms

A View Transform compresses the high dynamic range (HDR) of 3D scenes down to the low dynamic range (LDR) of standard displays. Without a proper transform—such as using the legacy Standard transform—any light value exceeding 1.0 clips immediately to pure white, blowing out highlights and causing harsh, unnatural transitions.

Modern transforms solve this by introducing an S-curve roll-off that preserves details in both dark shadows and bright highlights.

Filmic View Transform

Introduced to replace the Standard transform, Filmic expands Blender's dynamic range to approximately 16–25 stops of light.

  • Mechanics: Filmic applies a logarithmic tone curve that gradually compresses bright values rather than clipping them abruptly. This allows bright light sources, such as direct sunlight or fire, to roll off smoothly into highlights.
  • Limitations: While Filmic handles dynamic range well, it suffers from noticeable hue shifts and oversaturation in high-exposure areas. For instance, highly saturated and bright lights (like saturated LEDs or intense fire) often shift toward yellow or cyan before desaturating to white, producing an unnatural "abney effect."

AgX View Transform

Starting in Blender 4.0, AgX replaced Filmic as the default View Transform. Designed to address Filmic’s color fidelity issues, AgX offers an improved approach to highlight handling and color degradation.

  • Mechanics: AgX uses an advanced color-grading matrix combined with an S-curve tone-mapper. As colors become more intense and approach the limits of the display, AgX desaturates them gracefully toward white (chromaticity preservation), mimicking how real physical camera sensors and photographic film react to overexposure.
  • Advantages over Filmic: AgX avoids the harsh hue shifts seen in Filmic. Intense colored lights retain their intended hue throughout the gradient, and skin tones under bright lighting maintain natural coloration without skewing into sickly yellows or artificial tones.

Comparison: Standard vs. Filmic vs. AgX

Feature Standard Filmic AgX
Dynamic Range Low (Clips at 1.0) High (~16–25 stops) High (~16–25 stops)
Highlight Roll-off Harsh / Abrupt Smooth S-curve Smooth S-curve
Color Handling Clips to pure primary channels Shifts hue in overexposed areas Gracefully desaturates to white
Best Use Case Motion Graphics, UI, non-photorealistic rendering requiring exact hex codes Legacy projects Photorealistic rendering, complex lighting, VFX

Choosing the Right Transform

  • Use AgX for almost all photorealistic renders, physical simulations, architectural visualization, and VFX. It yields the most physically plausible and film-like result directly out of the viewport.
  • Use Filmic if you are maintaining consistency across older Blender projects or pipelines built specifically around Filmic color LUTs.
  • Use Standard only when working on non-photorealistic rendering (NPR), 2D motion design, or UI assets where exact color fidelity to specific hex codes or uncompressed textures is strictly required.
