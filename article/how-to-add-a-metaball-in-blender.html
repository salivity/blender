How to Add a Metaball in Blender

Metaballs in Blender are unique 3D objects that dynamically merge and blend into one another when brought into close proximity, making them ideal for creating fluid effects, organic shapes, and clay-like models. Adding a metaball to your project is a quick process that utilizes Blender’s standard object creation menus. This article provides a straightforward guide on how to add, configure, and convert metaballs in Blender.

Steps to Add a Metaball

  1. Open the 3D Viewport: Ensure you are in Object Mode. You can check your current mode in the top-left corner of the 3D Viewport.
  2. Open the Add Menu: Press Shift + A on your keyboard, or click Add in the top-left menu bar of the 3D Viewport.
  3. Select Metaball: Hover over the Metaball category in the drop-down menu.
  4. Choose a Shape: Click on one of the available base shapes:
    • Ball: A standard spherical metaball.
    • Capsule: A tube-like shape with rounded ends.
    • Plane: A flat rectangular metaball surface.
    • Ellipsoid: An elongated sphere.
    • Cube: A box-shaped metaball.

Once selected, the metaball will appear at the location of your 3D Cursor.

How Metaballs Work Together

When you duplicate a metaball (Shift + D) or add another metaball object while in Edit Mode, the objects will automatically fuse together whenever their outer boundaries intersect.

  • Object Mode vs. Edit Mode: Adding a new metaball while in Edit Mode keeps the new shape within the same family, sharing settings like resolution. Adding a metaball in Object Mode creates a separate object, though it will still interact with other metaballs as long as their names share the same prefix (e.g., MBall).

Adjusting Metaball Settings

To change how smooth or detailed your metaballs appear: 1. Select the metaball. 2. Go to the Object Data Properties tab (the green metaball icon on the right-hand panel). 3. Adjust the Resolution Viewport and Render values: * Lower values create a higher-density, smoother surface (requires more processing power). * Higher values create a lower-density, blockier surface for faster performance.

Converting a Metaball to a Standard Mesh

Because metaballs use procedural geometry, you cannot directly edit their individual vertices until you convert them to a regular mesh.

  1. Select your metaball structure in Object Mode.
  2. Right-click in the 3D Viewport to open the Object Context Menu.
  3. Choose Convert To > Mesh.

After converting, the metaball behavior stops, and the object turns into a standard 3D mesh ready for sculpting or traditional vertex editing.
