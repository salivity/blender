Color Grading in Blender Video Sequence Editor

Blender’s Video Sequence Editor (VSE) features built-in color grading tools that allow editors to balance footage, adjust exposure, and apply creative color styles directly on the timeline without switching to the Compositor or external software. This guide walks through the essential modifiers, scopes, and project settings required to achieve professional color correction and grading entirely within the Blender VSE.

1. Set Up Scene Color Management

Before adjusting individual video clips, establish proper project-wide color management settings:

  1. Open the Properties panel and navigate to the Render tab.
  2. Scroll down to the Color Management section.
  3. Set the Display Device to sRGB for standard web and video delivery.
  4. Set the View Transform to Standard for standard video footage, or AgX / Filmic if working with high dynamic range renders or raw footage.
  5. Use the Look dropdown to apply preset contrast levels (such as Medium High Contrast) if desired.

2. Enable Video Scopes for Accurate Monitoring

To evaluate luminance and chrominance accurately, use the built-in scopes:

  1. In the VSE Preview window, open the right-side properties sidebar (press N).
  2. Go to the View tab and locate the Scopes panel.
  3. Enable the scopes you need:
    • Luma Waveform: Monitors overall exposure, clipping (shadows at 0, highlights at 100).
    • RGB Parade: Displays red, green, and blue channels side-by-side to detect color casts.
    • Vectorscope: Displays color saturation and hue distribution to ensure skin tones align correctly along the skin-tone axis.

3. Add Color Modifiers to Video Strips

Color grading in the VSE is applied non-destructively using strip modifiers. Select your video strip in the timeline, press N to open the sidebar, and switch to the Modifiers tab.

Color Balance Modifier

The Color Balance modifier is the primary tool for primary and secondary color correction. It offers two modes:

  • Lift / Gamma / Gain:
    • Lift: Controls shadows (blacks).
    • Gamma: Controls midtones.
    • Gain: Controls highlights (whites).
  • Offset / Power / Slope (ASC-CDL standard):
    • Offset: Shifts the entire exposure up or down.
    • Power: Adjusts the midtone contrast.
    • Slope: Scales the highlights.

Click the color wheels to introduce tint into specific luminance ranges, or use the value slider beside each wheel to balance light levels.

Curves Modifier

Use the Curves modifier for precise tonal adjustments:

  • Use the C (Combined) curve to create an S-curve for added contrast.
  • Switch to the R, G, or B channels to balance specific color channels individually.

Hue Correct Modifier

Use the Hue Correct modifier to isolate and adjust specific colors:

  • Adjust saturation or hue for specific color ranges (e.g., boosting blues in the sky or desaturating distracting background elements).

Brightness/Contrast Modifier

A quick, global tool for simple exposure and dynamic range tweaks across the strip.

4. Grade Multiple Strips Using Adjustment Layers

To apply a cohesive look across multiple cuts without copying modifiers to every strip:

  1. Position the playhead over your edited sequence.
  2. In the Sequencer menu, click Add > Adjustment Layer.
  3. Place the Adjustment Layer on a channel above your video clips and stretch its handles across the entire edit.
  4. Select the Adjustment Layer, go to the Modifiers tab, and add your creative color grade (such as Color Balance or Curves). All video strips underneath the adjustment layer will inherit the grade.

5. Recommended Grading Workflow

  1. Normalize: Correct exposure and white balance strip-by-strip using the Color Balance modifier and scopes.
  2. Match: Ensure adjacent shots have matching skin tones and black levels.
  3. Creative Look: Add an Adjustment Layer across the timeline to apply global contrast, film looks, or stylized color tints.
