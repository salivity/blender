How to Enable Snapping During Transformations in Blender

Snapping in Blender allows you to precisely align objects, vertices, edges, or faces to specific points in your 3D viewport during movement, rotation, or scaling. You can enable snapping permanently by toggling the magnet icon at the top of the viewport or temporarily by holding the Ctrl key while performing a transformation. This guide covers how to quickly turn on snapping, change snapping modes, and use essential keyboard shortcuts for efficient 3D modeling.

Toggling Snapping On and Off

There are two primary ways to activate snapping in Blender:

  • UI Toggle: Click the Magnet icon located at the top center of the 3D Viewport header. When the icon is highlighted in blue, snapping is active.
  • Keyboard Shortcut: Press Shift + Tab anywhere in the 3D Viewport to toggle snapping on or off instantly.

Temporary Snapping Shortcut

If you only need to snap an object for a single transformation without turning the main setting on:

  1. Select your object or geometry.
  2. Press G (Move), R (Rotate), or S (Scale) to start the transformation.
  3. Press and hold the Ctrl key while moving your mouse. Blender will temporarily enable snapping as long as Ctrl is held down.
  4. Left-click to confirm the placement, then release Ctrl.

Note: If snapping is already toggled ON in the UI, holding Ctrl during a transformation will temporarily disable snapping.

Selecting Snap Targets

To choose what your selection snaps to, click the drop-down menu next to the Magnet icon in the top header (or press Shift + Ctrl + Tab):

  • Increment: Snaps to the background grid lines.
  • Vertex: Snaps to the nearest vertex of another mesh.
  • Edge: Snaps to the nearest edge line.
  • Face Project / Face Nearest: Snaps to the surface of a polygon face.
  • Volume: Snaps inside the 3D volume of another object.

Snapping Target Options

To fine-tune how the selection aligns with the target, adjust the Snap With options in the snapping menu:

  • Closest: Snaps the point of the selected object that is closest to the target.
  • Center: Snaps the median center or origin of the selection to the target.
  • Active: Snaps using the active vertex or element as the pivot point.
