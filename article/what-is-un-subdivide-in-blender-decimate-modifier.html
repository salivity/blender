What Is Un-Subdivide in Blender Decimate Modifier

The Un-Subdivide mode in Blender's Decimate modifier is a specialized tool designed to reverse the subdivision process on 3D meshes with regular, grid-like topology. Unlike other decimation methods that collapse edges randomly or merge planar faces, Un-Subdivide reduces polygon count by systematically removing grid lines while attempting to preserve clean, quad-based geometry. This article explains how Un-Subdivide works, its core settings, ideal use cases, and its limitations.

How Un-Subdivide Works

When a mesh is subdivided, each quad face is split into smaller quad faces. The Un-Subdivide mode reverses this operation by detecting grid patterns in your mesh's topology and removing alternating rows and columns of vertices.

Because it relies on structured geometric patterns, it works almost exclusively on meshes composed of clean, four-sided polygons (quads).

Core Settings

The Un-Subdivide mode features a simple interface with one main control parameter:

  • Iterations: Controls the number of times the un-subdivision pass is applied to the mesh.
    • Even Numbers (2, 4, 6...): Restores the original grid orientation and reduces density while keeping the edges parallel to the original structure.
    • Odd Numbers (1, 3, 5...): Rotates the grid pattern by 45 degrees, which creates a diagonal quad layout.

When to Use Un-Subdivide

Un-Subdivide is ideal for specific mesh optimization tasks where maintaining clean topology is crucial:

  1. Reversing Applied Subdivision: If a Subdivision Surface modifier was applied to a high-poly mesh, Un-Subdivide can often restore the lower-density base mesh without converting quad faces into triangles.
  2. Optimizing Uniform Quad Meshes: Terrain grids, cloth simulations, or imported CAD models with dense, uniform grid structures can be simplified quickly while keeping edge loops intact.
  3. Preparing Models for Animation: Because it preserves quad-based topology better than the Collapse or Planar modes, it helps maintain predictable mesh deformation for rigging and animation.

Limitations of Un-Subdivide

While powerful, Un-Subdivide is highly sensitive to mesh topology:

  • Requires Regular Grids: It will fail or produce distorted results on irregular meshes, triangulated geometry, or models with n-gons.
  • Sensitive to Poles: Vertices connected to more or fewer than four edges (poles) disrupt the grid pattern and may result in uneven decimation around those areas.
  • Does Not Target Flat Surface Simplification: If your goal is to reduce flat, planar areas regardless of grid flow, the Planar mode in the Decimate modifier is a better choice.
