What Is the Shrinkwrap Constraint in Blender?

The Shrinkwrap constraint in Blender is a powerful transformation tool that forces an object or bone to snap to or align with the surface of another target mesh. This article explains what the Shrinkwrap constraint is, how it functions dynamically during animation and modeling, its primary use cases, and how it differs from the Shrinkwrap modifier.

How the Shrinkwrap Constraint Works

Unlike static operations, the Shrinkwrap constraint dynamically recalculates an object’s position relative to a target mesh in real time. When applied to an object or bone, it projects that item onto the surface, boundary, or volume of a target object based on specific mathematical modes.

Key Wrap Modes

  • On Surface: Moves the constrained object to the closest point on the target mesh’s surface.
  • Project: Projects the object along a specified axis (X, Y, or Z) until it hits the target surface.
  • Nearest Surface Point: Snaps the object to the nearest point on the outer shell of the target.
  • Nearest Target Vertex: Snaps the object directly to the closest vertex of the target object.

Primary Uses for the Shrinkwrap Constraint

1. Character Rigging and Animation

Riggers frequently use the Shrinkwrap constraint on armature bones. For example, it can constrain an eye bone to move smoothly along the curved surface of an stylized eyeball, or keep facial controls attached to a deforming head mesh during complex expressions.

2. Attaching Accessories to Deforming Meshes

When a character deforms during animation, external items like clothing fasteners, buttons, armor plates, or bandage props can drift away from the body. Using a Shrinkwrap constraint keeps these objects stuck precisely to the underlying character skin without distorting the accessory’s geometry.

3. Decal and Graphic Alignment

Flat 3D meshes representing stickers, patches, or panel details can be constrained to curved surfaces like vehicles or rounded shields. This keeps the decal aligned flush with the target object even if the underlying object moves or morphs.

4. Sliding UI Controls and Constraints

For complex custom rigs, animators use the constraint to restrict control handles to move along specified 3D surfaces or curved planes, creating clean sliding control systems.

Shrinkwrap Constraint vs. Shrinkwrap Modifier

While both tools share similar projection algorithms, they serve different purposes:

  • Shrinkwrap Modifier: Deforms the actual geometry (vertices) of a mesh to fit another surface. It is primarily used for retopology, fitting clothing geometry, and modeling workflows.
  • Shrinkwrap Constraint: Moves the entire transform origin of an object or bone without deforming the underlying mesh structure. It is strictly used for object placement, tracking, and animation rigging.
