Blender Quick Favorites Menu Explained

The Quick Favorites menu in Blender is a fully customizable pop-up menu designed to give users instant access to their most frequently used tools, commands, and settings. By binding preferred operations to a single hotkey, this feature significantly speeds up 3D modeling workflows and reduces the need to navigate complex menus. This article explains what the Quick Favorites menu is, how to set it up, how it adapts to different modes, and why it is essential for an efficient Blender pipeline.

What is the Quick Favorites Menu?

The Quick Favorites menu is an empty, user-defined palette in Blender that acts as a shortcut hub. Instead of searching through top bar menus, search pop-ups, or sidebar panels, you can store any button, tool, or dropdown option directly inside this menu. Pressing the shortcut key Q opens the menu directly at your mouse cursor.

How to Add and Remove Shortcuts

Customizing the Quick Favorites menu takes only a few clicks:

  • To Add an Item: Right-click on almost any button, tool, or menu entry in Blender, and select Add to Quick Favorites.
  • To Remove an Item: Open the Quick Favorites menu by pressing Q, right-click the item you want to delete, and select Remove from Quick Favorites.

Context-Sensitive Functionality

A key feature of the Quick Favorites menu is its context sensitivity. The menu automatically changes its contents based on your current workspace and active mode.

For example, the items stored while working in Object Mode will remain separate from the items added while in Edit Mode, Sculpt Mode, or the Shader Editor. This ensures that the menu only displays relevant tools for your current task, keeping the interface uncluttered.

Why You Should Use It

  • Increases Speed: Access deep menu functions instantly without moving your cursor across the screen.
  • Reduces Memorization: Instead of learning dozens of complex keyboard shortcuts, you can group obscure tools into a single, easy-to-reach menu.
  • Streamlines Interface: Keeps your primary viewport clean by hiding secondary tools inside the pop-up panel.

The Quick Favorites menu saves automatically with your project or default Blender startup file, ensuring a seamless, tailored modeling experience across sessions.
