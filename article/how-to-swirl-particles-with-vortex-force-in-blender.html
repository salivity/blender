How to Swirl Particles with Vortex Force in Blender

Using a Vortex Force Field in Blender is the most effective way to create spiral, whirlpool, or tornado-like particle animations. This guide covers how to set up an emitter particle system, add and align the Vortex Force Field, adjust key parameters such as strength and inflow, and fine-tune the simulation to achieve a smooth swirling effect.

Step 1: Create the Particle Emitter

  1. Open Blender and ensure you are in the 3D Viewport.
  2. Add a mesh to act as your particle source by pressing Shift + A and selecting Mesh > Grid (or a Plane / UV Sphere).
  3. Navigate to the Particle Properties tab on the right panel (icon with three dots and lines).
  4. Click the + button to add a new particle system and set the type to Emitter.
  5. In the Emission settings:
    • Set the Number of particles (e.g., 1000 to 5000).
    • Set the Frame Start to 1 and End to 100.
    • Increase the Lifetime to 150 to ensure particles stay visible throughout the swirl.

Step 2: Add the Vortex Force Field

  1. Press Shift + A in the 3D Viewport.
  2. Hover over Force Field and select Vortex.
  3. Position the Vortex Force Field near or directly above your emitter mesh using the G key.

Step 3: Align the Vortex Axis

The Vortex force rotates particles around its local Z-axis.

  • To swirl particles horizontally around a central point, keep the Vortex upright (Z-axis pointing up).
  • To swirl particles horizontally or diagonally, select the Vortex and press R, followed by the axis key (X or Y), to rotate the field in the desired direction.

Step 4: Configure Vortex Settings

Select the Vortex object and go to the Physics Properties tab (icon with a bouncing ball around a ring):

  • Strength: Controls the rotational speed of the swirl. Positive values rotate particles counterclockwise, while negative values rotate them clockwise. Set this between 2.0 and 10.0 to start.
  • Inflow: Controls whether particles are pulled inward toward the center or pushed outward. A positive value pulls particles inward (like a whirlpool), while a negative value flings them outward.
  • Shape: Keep it set to Point for an omnidirectional field or change to Plane if you want the force distributed along a flat surface.
  • Falloff: In the Falloff section, adjust the Power to control how quickly the force weakens with distance. Set Power to 0 for an even force across the entire scene, or 2.0 for a realistic drop-off.

Step 5: Adjust Scene Gravity and Particle Dynamics

To prevent default gravity from pulling your particles straight down before they can swirl:

  1. Select your particle emitter mesh.
  2. Go to the Particle Properties tab.
  3. Expand the Field Weights panel.
  4. Reduce the Gravity slider from 1.0 to 0.0 (or a low value like 0.1).
  5. Set the Vortex weight slider to 1.0.

Step 6: Bake and Fine-Tune the Simulation

  1. Press the Spacebar to play the animation and preview the swirl.
  2. If particles move too fast or erratically, add a Drag Force Field (Shift + A > Force Field > Drag) to slow them down smoothly.
  3. Once satisfied with the motion, select your emitter, go to Particle Properties > Cache, and click Bake to lock in the simulation for rendering.
