How to Assign an IK Constraint to a Bone in Blender

Assigning an Inverse Kinematics (IK) constraint to a bone in Blender streamlines character rigging by allowing a chain of bones to automatically rotate and follow a single control bone. This tutorial provides a direct, step-by-step guide on how to add an IK constraint, set its target, adjust the chain length, and configure a pole target for precise animation control.

Step-by-Step Guide to Adding an IK Constraint

1. Prepare Your Armature

  1. Ensure your armature has an extra, unparented bone to act as the IK Controller (e.g., a foot or hand controller).
  2. Ensure you have a separate bone to act as the Pole Target if you need to control joint orientation (e.g., knee or elbow direction).

2. Add the IK Constraint

  1. Select your armature and switch to Pose Mode (Ctrl + Tab).
  2. Select the bone that should be driven by the IK (for example, the lower leg or forearm bone).
  3. Open the Bone Properties panel on the right sidebar and click the Bone Constraint Properties tab (the blue bone with a gear icon).
  4. Click Add Bone Constraint and select Inverse Kinematics from the tracking section.

3. Configure the IK Target

  1. In the IK constraint settings, click the Target field and select your Armature object.
  2. In the Bone field, select the control bone you created to drive the chain.

4. Adjust the Chain Length

  1. Locate the Chain Length slider in the constraint properties.
  2. Set the value to the exact number of bones you want the IK to affect.
    • Example: For a leg setup (thigh and lower leg), set the Chain Length to 2.
    • Setting the Chain Length to 0 affects all connected parent bones back to the root, which is usually undesirable.

5. Set Up the Pole Target (Optional)

  1. In the same IK constraint panel, click the Pole Target field and select your Armature object.
  2. In the corresponding Bone field, select your elbow or knee control bone.
  3. Adjust the Pole Angle slider (usually -90°, 90°, or 180°) if the mesh twists abnormally after assigning the pole target.

Alternative Shortcut Method

  1. Enter Pose Mode.
  2. Select the IK Controller bone first.
  3. Hold Shift and select the bone receiving the IK constraint.
  4. Press Shift + Ctrl + C to open the Quick Constraints menu.
  5. Select Inverse Kinematics.
  6. Select the receiving bone, navigate to the Bone Constraint Properties tab, and manually adjust the Chain Length.
