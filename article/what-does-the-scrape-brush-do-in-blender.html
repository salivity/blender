What Does the Scrape Brush Do in Blender?

The Scrape brush in Blender is a vital digital sculpting tool designed to flatten surfaces by shaving off high points relative to an area plane. It is primarily used for hard-surface sculpting, creating sharp edges, building stylized assets like rocks or hair, and refining organic forms into clean, planar surfaces. By pulling geometry down toward a flat target surface, it allows artists to create controlled bevels, chamfers, and defined angles on 3D meshes.

How the Scrape Brush Works

When you apply the Scrape brush to a mesh, Blender calculates an average plane based on the normal vectors of the vertices beneath the brush center. The brush then actively pushes down any geometry that extends above this calculated plane. Unlike the Flatten brush, which affects both high and low areas simultaneously, the default Scrape brush focuses exclusively on trimming down the peaks.

Key Functions and Features

  • Shaving High Points: The primary action is removing excess volume from raised areas, effectively sanding down high geometry until it aligns with the brush's plane.
  • Creating Sharp Edges: By scraping two adjacent curved or rough surfaces, you can create a distinct, crisp crest where the two planes meet.
  • Plane Offset Adjustment: You can adjust the height of the scraping plane. Increasing or decreasing the offset allows you to dig deeper into the mesh or lightly trim the surface.
  • Invert Action (Fill): Holding Ctrl while using the Scrape brush reverses its behavior, causing it to fill in valleys and low points up to the calculated plane rather than pushing high points down.

Common Use Cases in Sculpting

1. Hard-Surface Sculpting

The Scrape brush is essential for non-organic modeling, such as armor, vehicles, weapons, and mechanical components. It helps establish clean, flat panels and sharp, manufactured edges.

2. Stylized Environments and Assets

When sculpting stylized rocks, cliffs, or anime-style hair, artists rely heavily on the Scrape brush. It allows for the easy creation of the hard, planar facets characteristic of stylized 3D art.

3. Cleaning Up Rough Sculpts

During early dynamic topology (DynTopo) or high-density remesh stages, geometry can become noisy. The Scrape brush quickly smooths out messy areas into predictable planes without destroying the underlying silhouette.

Scrape vs. Flatten vs. Fill

  • Scrape: Pushes high points down to the area plane. Leaves lower areas intact.
  • Flatten: Moves both high points down and low points up to meet at an average height.
  • Fill: Pulls low points up to the area plane without affecting the high points.
