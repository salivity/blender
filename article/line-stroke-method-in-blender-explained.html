Line Stroke Method in Blender Explained

The line stroke method in Blender refers to the various techniques and settings used to control how brush strokes, lines, and textures are applied across 3D surfaces and 2D canvases. Primarily utilized in Grease Pencil drawing, Texture Painting, and Sculpting, selecting the right stroke method determines the placement, spacing, and smoothness of your input. This guide breaks down the core stroke methods available in Blender and explains how to use them effectively for precise artistic control.

What is a Stroke Method in Blender?

In Blender, a stroke method dictates how mouse or stylus input is translated into digital strokes. Instead of merely following your cursor continuously, Blender offers multiple algorithms to space out, anchor, or smooth lines depending on your specific project needs.

You can access stroke method settings in the Tool Settings bar or the Properties Panel under the Stroke tab when using painting, drawing, or sculpting brushes.

Primary Line Stroke Methods

1. Space

The Space method applies brush dabs along a path with a specified distance (spacing) between each mark. * How it works: As you drag your cursor, dabs are placed at regular intervals calculated as a percentage of the brush size. * Best used for: Continuous linework, dashed patterns, and smooth painting.

2. Line

The Line stroke method constrains your input to a perfectly straight line between two points. * How it works: Clicking defines the starting point; dragging establishes the direction and length; releasing the mouse/pen draws the final stroke. * Best used for: Hard-surface details, architectural outlines, and mechanical drawing in Grease Pencil.

3. Curve

The Curve method allows you to define a stroke using a Bezier curve path before applying the actual paint or Grease Pencil line. * How it works: You create control points to define a smooth curve on the canvas, then execute the stroke along that exact vector. * Best used for: Complex, ultra-smooth curves that are difficult to draw freehand.

4. Dots

The Dots method draws dabs based directly on user input speed without distance calculation. * How it works: Fast movement creates widely spaced dots, while slow movement creates a dense, continuous line. * Best used for: Quick sketches, organic texturing, and dynamic speed-based strokes.

5. Drag Dot

Drag Dot places a single brush mark on the surface that you can reposition before finalizing. * How it works: Clicking creates a single texture or brush dab, and dragging allows you to move it around the canvas until you release the button. * Best used for: Placing precise single details, such as rivets, blemishes, or specific decals on a 3D model.

6. Anchored

The Anchored method creates a single brush stroke that scales and rotates dynamically from a central point. * How it works: Click to set the center point, then drag outward to expand, shrink, or rotate the stroke shape. * Best used for: Stenciling, applying radial textures, and stamping geometric details.

Enhancing Line Precision with Stroke Stabilization

For artists using freehand methods like Space or Dots, Blender includes a Stabilize Stroke feature (often referred to as a “Lazy Mouse”). Enabling this adds a virtual drag-line behind your cursor that smooths out hand tremors and sharp jitter, ensuring clean, steady lines.

Summary of When to Use Each Method

  • Use Line and Curve for technical, hard-surface, and precise vector-like drawings.
  • Use Space and Dots for freehand sketching, shading, and general painting.
  • Use Drag Dot and Anchored for placing localized textures, stamps, and details.
