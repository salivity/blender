How to Use Mesh Filters in Blender Sculpt Mode

Applying a sculpting effect evenly across an entire 3D model in Blender is achieved using the Mesh Filter tool. Located in Sculpt Mode, this feature allows artists to apply global operations like smoothing, relaxing, inflating, or eroding across the whole mesh or unmasked areas simultaneously without manual brush strokes. This guide explains how to select, configure, and execute a mesh filter in Blender to speed up your 3D modeling workflow.

Step 1: Select the Mesh Filter Tool

  1. Select your object and switch to Sculpt Mode using the mode dropdown menu in the top-left corner of the viewport (or press Ctrl + Tab and select Sculpt).
  2. Open the Toolbar on the left side of the 3D Viewport by pressing T if it is not visible.
  3. Locate and click on the Mesh Filter tool icon (looks like a sphere with a grid overlaid).

Step 2: Choose Your Filter Type

Once the Mesh Filter tool is selected, you can choose the specific operation you want to apply across the mesh.

  1. Look at the Tool Settings bar at the top of the viewport (or open the Sidebar by pressing N and go to the Tool tab).
  2. Click the Filter Type dropdown menu.
  3. Choose the operation appropriate for your task:
    • Smooth: Evens out surface noise across the mesh.
    • Relax: Redistributes vertices evenly along the surface without altering the overall shape.
    • Inflate / Sharpen: Expands geometry outward or sharpens existing features.
    • Surface Smooth: Preserves sharp edges while smoothing flat surfaces.
    • Erase Multires: Resets displacement on objects using the Multiresolution modifier.

Step 3: Apply the Filter to the Mesh

  1. Click anywhere inside the 3D Viewport.
  2. Drag your mouse to the right to apply and increase the strength of the selected filter across the entire mesh.
  3. Drag your mouse to the left to decrease or invert the effect.
  4. Release the mouse button to confirm the operation.

Step 4: Restrict the Filter Area (Optional)

If you do not want the filter to affect the entire mesh, you can limit its scope using masks or face sets:

  • Masking: Paint a mask over areas you want to protect (press M to switch to the Mask brush). The Mesh Filter will automatically ignore masked regions.
  • Face Sets: Enable Deformation Domain in the tool settings and set it to Face Sets if you only want to affect a specific active Face Set.
