How to Extrude Along Normals in Blender

Extruding along normals in Blender allows you to extend faces directly along the direction they are facing, rather than along the global X, Y, or Z axes. This feature is essential for working with curved objects, complex angles, or multiple non-parallel faces simultaneously. This guide provides quick, step-by-step methods using keyboard shortcuts and tools to extrude geometry along face normals efficiently.

Method 1: The Extrude Menu Shortcut (Fastest)

  1. Enter Edit Mode: Select your 3D object and press Tab to switch to Edit Mode.
  2. Select Faces: Switch to Face Select Mode (press 3) and select the face or multiple faces you wish to extrude.
  3. Open Extrude Menu: Press Alt + E on your keyboard to bring up the Extrude menu.
  4. Choose Extrude Option: Select Extrude Faces Along Normals.
  5. Adjust Distance: Move your mouse to set the extrusion distance, or type an exact numerical value.
  6. Confirm: Left-click or press Enter to apply the extrusion.

Method 2: Using the Toolbar Tool

  1. Enter Edit Mode: Press Tab with your object selected.
  2. Activate the Tool: On the left toolbar, click and hold the Extrude Region icon until a sub-menu appears.
  3. Select Extrude Along Normals: Choose the Extrude Along Normals tool from the flyout menu.
  4. Drag the Handle: Select your faces and click-and-drag the yellow handle that appears on the mesh to extrude along the face normals visually.

Fine-Tuning the Extrusion (Offset Even)

When extruding multiple connected faces along their normals, corners can sometimes become distorted. To fix uneven geometry:

  1. Perform the extrusion using one of the methods above.
  2. Look at the bottom-left corner of the 3D Viewport to open the Extrude Faces Along Normals operator panel (or press F9).
  3. Enable the Offset Even checkbox. This forces Blender to recalculate the vertices so that the thickness remains uniform across all faces.
