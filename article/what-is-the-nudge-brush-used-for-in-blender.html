What Is the Nudge Brush Used for in Blender?

In Blender’s Sculpt Mode, the Nudge brush is a specialized manipulation tool designed to slide vertices along the surface of a mesh without altering its general volume or depth. This article covers the fundamental purpose of the Nudge brush, how it differs from similar sculpt tools, its most common practical applications, and the best settings to use for optimal mesh control.

How the Nudge Brush Works

The Nudge brush moves geometry in the direction of your brush stroke, effectively dragging the surface vertices parallel to your view angle. Unlike standard sculpting brushes that add or subtract volume (such as Draw or Clay), the Nudge brush simply shifts existing detail across the surface.

While the Grab brush pulls geometry outward or inward in 3D space—often causing volume distortion—the Nudge brush keeps the underlying surface shape intact while repositioning the topology.

Key Use Cases for the Nudge Brush

  • Adjusting Surface Features: Move fine details like wrinkles, scars, cloth folds, or facial features slightly to the left, right, up, or down without flattening or inflating the mesh.
  • Tuning Topology Flow: Shift crowded vertices apart or align edge loops smoothly along a curved surface without disrupting the silhouette of your model.
  • Correcting Proportions: Make micro-adjustments to the positioning of sculpt elements (such as sliding an eyelid, lip corner, or armor seam) with high precision.
  • Clearing Distortion: Fix localized pinching or stretched polygons by gently sliding vertices back into an even distribution.

Essential Settings for the Nudge Brush

To get the most control out of the Nudge brush, adjust these standard settings in the Tool Settings panel:

  1. Strength: Keeps movement subtle. A lower strength (around 0.2 to 0.5) prevents vertices from overlapping or creating non-manifold geometry.
  2. Radius: Sets the area of influence. A smaller radius is ideal for precise detail shifting, while a larger radius moves broader surface regions evenly.
  3. Auto Smooth: Enabling a slight amount of auto-smooth helps keep the surface topology clean as you drag vertices across the mesh.
