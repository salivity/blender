What Does the Solidify Modifier Do in Blender?

The Solidify modifier in Blender takes flat surfaces or open 3D meshes and adds depth to them by extruding the geometry to create a thick, solid object. This non-destructive tool allows artists to transform zero-thickness planes into realistic, three-dimensional objects like walls, clothing, containers, and vehicle shells without permanently altering the base mesh. This article explains how the modifier works, its essential settings, the difference between its operational modes, and its practical applications in 3D modeling.

Core Functionality of the Solidify Modifier

At its most basic level, the Solidify modifier duplicates the faces of a mesh, offsets them along their normal directions, and connects the edges of the original mesh to the duplicated mesh. This creates the illusion of volume and physical wall thickness on objects that would otherwise be infinitely thin.

Because it is a non-destructive modifier, you can adjust, enable, or disable the thickness at any point during your workflow without manually editing individual vertices.

Key Settings and Controls

Understanding the main properties of the Solidify modifier helps you achieve accurate results for different geometry types:

  • Thickness: Controls the distance between the original surface and the newly generated surface. Positive or negative values dictate the depth.
  • Offset: Determines where the thickness is applied relative to the original mesh. An offset of -1.0 extrudes inward, 1.0 extrudes outward, and 0.0 distributes the thickness evenly on both sides.
  • Even Thickness: Forces Blender to maintain a uniform wall thickness across sharp corners and steep angles, preventing the mesh from looking distorted or squeezed at tight turns.
  • Fill Rim: Fills the gap between the original perimeter edges and the newly generated surface edges, closing the mesh to make it watertight.
  • Only Rim: Generates only the side walls (the rim) connecting the original and extruded boundaries, removing the main faces. This is useful for creating hollow frames or borders.
  • Material Offsets: Allows you to assign different material slots to the generated rim and back faces without splitting the mesh.

Simple Mode vs. Complex Mode

The Solidify modifier offers two primary modes depending on your geometric needs:

  1. Simple Mode: The default option. It is fast, efficient, and ideal for standard, clean geometry like planes, cutouts, or basic low-poly shapes.
  2. Complex Mode: Designed to handle non-manifold geometry, self-intersecting meshes, and complex shapes where Simple Mode produces overlaps or artifacts. It offers additional options for handling custom thickness constraints and boundary shapes.

Common Use Cases in Blender

  • Architecture: Quickly turn 2D floorplan planes into thick 3D walls.
  • Character & Apparel Modeling: Give realistic depth to clothes, armor, capes, and fabrics.
  • Hard Surface & Industrial Design: Add realistic metal or plastic panel thickness to vehicle bodies, helmets, and electronic casings.
  • 3D Printing: Prepare hollow or surface-only models for 3D printing by giving them the minimum wall thickness required for physical manufacturing.
